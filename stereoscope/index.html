Skylight Stereoscope

A Brewster-pattern box viewer for front-lit cards, in half-inch walnut, on a ten-inch pedestal. Drag to turn it; select any part for its size and file.

Cutting the parts…

    Optics

    Your two 38 mm, f 150 mm lenses sit 78 mm apart, wider than your eyes. You look through the inner half of each, which draws the two pictures together the way the prism lenses of a Holmes viewer do. The card stands 134 to 148 mm from the lenses, just inside the focal length, so the picture floats between 1.3 m and 11 m away.

    Each eyepiece turns on a four-start thread, 12 mm to the turn, and they focus separately.

    Light

    The skylight is a trapezoid of ground glass in the sloping top, tilted 10° toward the card. A gilded sill at the foot of the card throws light back up to its lower edge; the lit face is turned away from the lenses, so you never see it. Light from above reflects downward off glossy albumen, away from your eyes.

    A 3 mm septum keeps each eye to its own half, and keeps the two projected pictures apart on the front glass.

    Mount

    Under the viewer is a walnut socle with a tapered socket. On the capital stands a matching key: 64 × 40 mm at the root, 8° a side, 20 mm tall. Lower the viewer and it centres itself. Tip it and the key wedges in the socket within about three degrees, so it cannot be knocked off; it leaves only straight up, and a pair of 12 mm magnets holds it against that until you mean it.

    Off the pedestal the viewer stands level on the socle.

    Storage

    The drawer pulls toward you and holds about thirty cards lying flat, standard or cabinet size, on a ribbon that lifts the stack. The front bay is for cotton gloves and a lens cloth.

    The cover has its own place: a felt-lined groove on the plinth where it stands at 8°, relief toward you, while you look.

    Parts and files

    Every solid is modelled at full size in millimetres and exported lying flat, ready to slice or to send to a router. Files arrive zipped, because this page can only hand over a zip. Sizes below are the bounding box of the part as exported.

    Shop notes

    • Stock. Walnut planed to 12.7 mm. The sides splay 8° in plan and the top falls 10°; every joint is a single bevel, no compound mitres.
    • Eyepiece holes. 54 mm (2⅛ in Forstner), 78 mm between centres, 47 mm above the floor.
    • Glass. 3 mm ground glass, ground face inward. Front pane mm. Skylight is a trapezoid, mm overall; cut it from the file.
    • Inside. Blacken the walls, lens board and septum. Gild or silver only the sloping face of the sill.
    • Felt. Line both faces of the card slot, the socket, the cover groove and the drawer. In the socket it also takes up the 0.4 mm clearance.
    • Magnets. Two 12 × 3 mm discs (key and floor), eight 6 × 3 mm (cover and skylight frame). Check polarity before gluing.
    • Pedestal. An M8 rod runs from under the plinth top through the column and capital; the key screws on over the nut. A 6 mm steel plate under the plinth keeps it planted when you lean on the eyecups.
    • Printing. Sleeve, barrel and ring print without supports as exported. Threads have 0.25 mm radial clearance; scale the barrel to 99% across X and Y if yours bind.
    • Relief. The sunburst is a stand-in for your own panel. The pocket is mm, 4.5 mm deep.
    • Cards. The slot takes mounts up to 4 × 7 in, flat or curved. The card overlaps each wall by 3 mm, which is what registers it.
