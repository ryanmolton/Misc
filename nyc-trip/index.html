Where to stay

Two blocks from the park, very British

Our pick

The Whitby Hotel

18 West 56th Street, between Fifth & Sixth Avenues

A Firmdale hotel designed by Kit Kemp, the most London-feeling place to sleep in New York. The Orangery downstairs serves afternoon tea every day, which makes a perfect rainy-afternoon backup. The location is the real reason: Days 1 and 4 happen entirely on foot, and every evening is a short stroll home to the cot.

Day 0

Land, check in, and a first look

  • The flight. San Diego to JFK or Newark is about 5½ hours nonstop. Feed him on takeoff and landing to help his ears.
  • Airport to hotel. Bring his infant car seat and book a car you can install it in. Uber's car-seat option only fits older toddlers, and it's 45–75 minutes into Manhattan.
  • Evening stroll (7 min). Walk down Fifth Avenue to Rockefeller Center to see the lights and the Channel Gardens, then back for dinner at The Whitby Bar or room service by the window.
  • His clock. Keep him on San Diego time. His 7pm bedtime is 10pm here, so late breakfasts, a later evening, and no alarm clocks.
Day 5

Bagels, one last stroll, fly home

  • Breakfast. Hand-rolled bagels with lox from Ess-a-Bagel on Third Avenue, eaten on a bench at Grand Army Plaza, where Day 1 began.
  • Leave early. Allow a relaxed 3 hours before the flight for traffic, the car seat, and a diaper change at the gate.
  • Westbound bonus. Flying home, the three hours come back, and a late flight lines up with his San Diego bedtime.

The Broadway question

Broadway, the baby-proof way

The honest answer first: Broadway theaters don't admit babies. Nearly every house requires a ticket for every person and sets a minimum age of around four. So here are three ways to still get a show.

Recommended · Day 3 night

A proper date night

He has napped, had his bath and is in pajamas when a licensed sitter (booked through The Whitby concierge) arrives at 6:30. Walk ten minutes to the theater district.

Her show: SIX. The West End hit about Henry VIII's six wives, staged as a pop concert. Eighty minutes with no intermission, so you're home by 9:30. Check it's still playing for your dates; TodayTix and the TKTS booth in Times Square sell same-day seats.

Theater he can come to

New York City Children's Theater stages "Theater for the Very Young" shows for ages 0–5: soft light, gentle music, and babies welcome to wiggle.

Nitehawk Cinema, Prospect Park runs "Adults with Infants" Tuesday matinees: a new-release film with dinner served, and crying fully allowed.

Both run on seasonal schedules. Check what's on for your dates.

Broadway from the sidewalk

At dusk, stroll Shubert Alley and West 44th Street as the marquees light up. It's 10 minutes from the hotel, and the stage door after a matinee is fun even for a six-month-old.

Making it easy

The little things

Stroller and carrier, both
A light folding stroller for the long walks, plus the carrier for the bridge crowds, St. Patrick's, and subway stairs.
Cabs over subways
Most subway stations have stairs and no elevator. Every cab hop in this plan is 10–25 minutes.
Changing and feeding
Museums, the library and big hotels have the nicest rooms. The plan passes one every hour or two.
Naps are the schedule
Each day has a stroller-nap stretch in the late morning and a hotel nap in the afternoon. If he's done, skip the last stop. The best parts come first.
Weather
Layers, a sun hat and a stroller rain cover. If it pours, swap in tea in The Whitby Orangery and The Met's Temple of Dendur (cab, 10 min).
