New York City · five nights · on foot & by subway

Our Little New Yorker

Four slow, beautiful days for the three of you. Tea under a stained-glass ceiling, his first carousel ride, the Guggenheim's spiral at stroller speed, and sunset twice: once from the 70th floor, once from the Brooklyn waterfront. Every leg is walkable or a subway ride, with times at an unhurried stroller pace.

Where we'll stay

One home base for all five nights, so the crib gets set up once and every morning starts with a walk into the park.

Home base · Midtown, by Central Park

The Whitby Hotel

18 West 56th Street, between Fifth & Sixth
  • Six minutes to Central Park's southeast corner. Most mornings start with a stroll, not a commute.
  • Designed by Kit Kemp: color, pattern and art in every room. It feels like a very chic friend's townhouse.
  • Afternoon tea in the hotel's own Orangery, a second tea for a rainy afternoon without leaving the building.
  • Near the F at 57 St and the B/D/F/M at Rockefeller Center, plus a flat walk to Grand Central for the airport train.

When you book

Ask for a crib and a high floor away from the elevator bank. Reserve afternoon tea at The Plaza and dinner at The River Café at the same time, since both book up.

If The Whitby is full

The Lowell on the Upper East Side is quieter and residential, a few blocks from the Met. The Beekman in Lower Manhattan puts you at the Brooklyn Bridge end of the plan, with a soaring Victorian atrium she'll love.

Day by day

Mornings are for the big sights while he's fresh. Afternoons leave room for a stroller nap on a long walk, then sunset. Times are loose; the order is the point.

Showtime, baby-sized

Broadway theaters require a ticket for every guest and generally don't admit infants, so the theater moments here are built around him.

Plug into Day 3 morning

New Victory Theater, 42nd Street

New York's theater for kids and families sits right on the Broadway strip, a few doors from Times Square. It regularly programs short, gentle shows made for babies, with soft light, music, and floor seating where wiggling and babbling are part of the performance. Check the calendar for your dates and book the earliest show; it's a five-minute walk from the Times Sq–42 St stop on Day 3.

Free, every day

The bronze orchestra at the zoo

The Delacorte Musical Clock above the Central Park Zoo gate plays nursery rhymes every half hour while a bear, a hippo, a goat and friends parade around it. Plan Day 1 so you're standing under it on the hour, when the song runs longest.

Every evening

A Broadway marquee walk

Walk Shubert Alley and 44th and 45th Streets after dinner, when the marquees light up and the crowds go inside. It's two blocks off the Day 3 route and eight minutes from the hotel.

If you're here July–August

Broadway in Bryant Park

Casts from current Broadway shows perform free on the Bryant Park lawn at lunchtime. Bring a blanket, sit near the back, and slip out whenever he needs to.

Getting around, car-free

No cabs or ride-shares with his car seat question hanging over them. Just your feet, a stroller, a carrier, and the subway.

Arriving

JFK by train

AirTrain from your terminal to Jamaica, then the LIRR to Grand Central Madison, about an hour door to platform. Both have elevators the whole way, and the LIRR has room for a stroller and bags. Flying into Newark instead? AirTrain to NJ Transit to Penn Station takes about 35 minutes.

The key gear

Stroller + carrier

Many subway stations are stairs-only. The stations on this plan are ones with elevators, except York St on Day 4. Wear him in the carrier for those, fold the stroller, and it's easy. The carrier is also better for the Brooklyn Bridge and Top of the Rock.

Fares

Tap and go

Tap a phone or card at any turnstile (OMNY). He rides free: children under 44 inches don't pay. Use the wide service gate with the stroller, and stand by the doors where there's room.

Timing trick

Keep him on San Diego time

A 7 pm bedtime at home is 10 pm here. For a short trip, letting him stay mostly on Pacific time means sunsets and early dinners fit naturally, and mornings start a little later.

Pace

How times are measured

Walk times assume about 2.5 mph with a stroller and no stops. The longest single walk is the 35-minute Brooklyn Bridge crossing, and every day has a hotel break or a stroller-nap stretch built in.

Changing & feeding

Where to pause

The Met, the Guggenheim, Chelsea Market, and The Plaza all have clean restrooms. Bryant Park's are famously lovely, with fresh flowers and an attendant. Park benches along the Literary Walk and the High Line are good for a quiet feed.

Hours, elevator service, show calendars and reservations change. Check the MTA's elevator status and each place's site the week before you fly. Walk times are estimates from the map, not from turn-by-turn routing.
